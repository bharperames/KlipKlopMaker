Track geometry · STANDARD 🔒
Canonical part geometry — the numbers below are fixed, so any two parts carrying the same code mate: slope 11.22° (30 mm drop per tile incl. the 0.25 mm waterfall seam), curves R 143.6 mm (45 mm drop), channel 48 mm, supports on a 15 mm riser grid. Designs loaded with other geometry are re-laid on this standard and flagged.
Every exported part is engraved with its code and this version. The version is a compatibility claim: parts with different MAJOR numbers are not expected to fit each other. Joints changed in 2.0 — the track socket, the key pocket and the gate pivot — so 2.0 parts do not mate with 1.x parts.

      Print settings (research-derived)

      Material: PLA (rigid → loud, sharp clack)
      Walls & infill: barely matter — the track is a 2.4 mm shell, six lines wide at a 0.4 nozzle, so it prints solid whatever you set
      Track: as oriented, and the orientation is part of the design. Viaduct pieces stand rim-down on the arcade, no supports. Minimal straights and lifts come out already tilted onto their own underside — lay them down as exported and do not re-orient them
      Curves & switches: one part per plate. The cantilever warning fires on them regardless, so alone it is attributable and a failure costs one part
      Cooling: run it full. The arch crowns and the walking surface are both bridged across open air

      Connection Guide

      Bowtie Joint Anatomy

      Track pieces connect using a separate, flat-printed bowtie key. This provides key benefits:

      • Zero Overhangs: Support-free printing for track pieces & keys.
      • Tension Lock: The tip is wider than the mouth it would have to pass back through, so the seam cannot pull apart.
      • Seat Register: The key's top face lands on the pocket ceiling in both pieces, and both ceilings sit 3 mm under their own deck — so seating the key is what makes the two walking surfaces coplanar.
      • Flank Grip: The pocket flanks close 0.03 mm over the last 4 mm of travel, then hold that section for a 1.5 mm land. Tightening the flanks pulls the seam shut, and the land lets the key be pushed home against the ceiling instead of stalling in a wedge.
      Connection Stages
      1. Alignment: Bring track ends face-to-face.
      2. Insertion: Align the bowtie key underneath the seam.
      3. Seat & Lock: Push the key all the way up until it stops against the pocket ceiling. The flanks grip over the last of the travel and hold the faces butted.
